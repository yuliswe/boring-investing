# Retail companies — analytical method

This method applies to companies that sell physical goods, mainly through their
own stores and websites. It takes a **franchise-quality** approach rather than a
discounted-cash-flow model. The goal is to judge whether the business has a
durable advantage that sustains high returns on capital, and whether the price
already assumes that it does.

For a fashion or apparel retailer the advantage is the brand, and customer
taste can turn within a few years. The metrics below are chosen to show when a
brand is weakening before the income statement does, and to avoid paying for
growth that depends on the brand staying in fashion.

The metrics are grouped into six sections, in the order a reader should take
them. Each one appears on the page with a popover that repeats its calculation
and what to watch for.

## 1. Valuation

| Metric             | Calculation                                                    | What to watch for                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| P/E ratio          | Year-end price ÷ diluted EPS                                   | Compare it with the stock's own history. Trough years push the ratio up because earnings collapse, so a high P/E in a bad year does not by itself mean the stock is expensive. |
| P/FCF after leases | Year-end price ÷ free cash flow after leases per diluted share | A ratio well below its own median suggests the price assumes little growth.                                                                                                    |

## 2. Demand and brand health

| Metric                             | Calculation                                                                | What to watch for                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Comparable sales growth            | As reported: sales growth at stores open more than a year, plus e-commerce | Comps below inflation for several years mean the brand is losing pull. Revenue growth that comes only from new stores while comps stall is a warning. |
| Revenue share of the growth market | Revenue from the expansion region ÷ total revenue                          | This share should keep rising while the expansion works. A stall means the growth story is fading.                                                    |
| Digital share of revenue           | Digital revenue ÷ total revenue                                            | A rising share is usually healthy, but digital orders carry shipping and return costs that stores do not.                                             |

## 3. Pricing power and inventory discipline

These three metrics describe one sequence. Overbuying shows up first in
inventory, it then turns into markdowns, and the markdowns finally show up in
gross margin.

| Metric                              | Calculation                                                                               | What to watch for                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gross margin                        | (Revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ revenue                                                  | A fall of 100 bp or more alongside talk of markdowns signals lost pricing power. When occupancy sits in cost of goods sold, gross margin also rises with sales volume, so part of any gain is operating leverage rather than pricing. |
| Inventory turnover                  | Cost of goods sold ÷ average of opening and closing inventory                             | A steady decline means merchandise is moving more slowly. Compare the level with the company's own history, since accounting choices change it.                                                                                       |
| Inventory growth minus sales growth | Year-over-year % change in year-end inventory − year-over-year % change in revenue, in pp | A gap above roughly +10 pp usually leads to markdowns within two or three quarters. This is the earliest warning in the sector.                                                                                                       |

## 4. Store growth and economics

| Metric                    | Calculation                                                        | What to watch for                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Store count               | As reported at fiscal year-end                                     | A flat home-market count is fine while international markets are early, but it shifts the growth story to comps.                                         |
| Net store openings        | Year-end count − prior year-end count                              | An accelerating pace needs stable revenue per store behind it.                                                                                           |
| Revenue per average store | Store-channel revenue ÷ average of opening and closing store count | This stands in for sales per square foot. It should hold or rise as the fleet grows. A fall means new stores are weaker or are cannibalizing older ones. |
| Capex as % of revenue     | Capital expenditures ÷ revenue                                     | A spike signals an expansion push, which should show up later in store count and revenue.                                                                |

## 5. Margins

These are shown as one multi-line chart, because all of them are shares of
revenue and the gaps between the lines carry the story.

| Metric                  | Calculation                           | What to watch for                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Operating margin        | Operating income ÷ revenue            | Judge it against the mid-cycle level rather than the peak.                                                                         |
| Net margin              | Net income ÷ revenue                  | The gap to operating margin is financing, other income and tax. One-off gains can flatter it.                                      |
| FCF margin after leases | Free cash flow after leases ÷ revenue | When this line falls below net margin, earnings are not turning into cash, usually because inventory is absorbing working capital. |

## 6. Returns and capital allocation

| Metric                     | Calculation                                                                                         | What to watch for                                                                                                                            |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Lease-adjusted ROIC        | Operating income × (1 − effective tax rate) ÷ average of (equity + debt + lease liabilities − cash) | A level sustained above about 15% points to a real franchise. A decline while the store base grows means new stores earn less than old ones. |
| Diluted EPS                | As reported                                                                                         | Compare its growth with net income growth. The difference comes from buybacks and dilution.                                                  |
| FCF per share after leases | Free cash flow after leases ÷ diluted shares                                                        | Over a cycle this should track diluted EPS.                                                                                                  |
| FCF conversion             | Free cash flow after leases ÷ net income                                                            | It should sit near or above 100% over a cycle. Single years swing with inventory.                                                            |
| Diluted share count        | Diluted weighted average shares                                                                     | Buybacks should shrink the count. A rising count means dilution outpaces repurchases.                                                        |
| Net cash                   | Cash − bank debt, excluding lease liabilities                                                       | This is the cushion that lets a retailer survive a product miss without diluting shareholders or halting expansion.                          |

## Free cash flow after leases

Store leases work like debt, so every free-cash-flow metric subtracts lease
principal payments:

> Free cash flow after leases = operating cash flow − capital expenditures −
> lease principal payments

Under IFRS 16, lease principal is reported in financing cash flow, so reported
free cash flow leaves most of the rent bill out. The subtraction puts it back,
and it keeps the series comparable with years before IFRS 16, when rent was
expensed inside operating cash flow. Under US GAAP, operating lease payments
stay in operating cash flow, so reported free cash flow already covers rent and
no adjustment is needed. The same logic is why returns are measured with
lease-adjusted ROIC rather than ROE: ROE ignores lease liabilities, and
buybacks inflate it by shrinking equity.

## What we do not do

- **No DCF or target price.** The page presents data and a qualitative thesis,
  not a valuation or a buy/sell recommendation.
- **No quarterly tracking.** Figures are fiscal-year snapshots, and quarterly
  noise is left out on purpose.
- **No peer comparison.** Each company is assessed on its own merits.
  Cross-company comparison belongs to a screen, not to a company page.
