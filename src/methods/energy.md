# Oil and gas producers — analytical method

This method applies to companies that find, develop and produce crude oil,
bitumen and natural gas and sell it at prices set by world and regional
markets. It takes a **through-the-cycle** approach rather than a
discounted-cash-flow model. The goal is to judge whether the business earns an
adequate return on its capital at mid-cycle prices, whether it grows the
production and reserves that belong to each share, and whether the balance
sheet survives the bottom of a price cycle.

## Why the usual metrics need care

A producer is a price taker, so most of the year-to-year change in revenue,
earnings and cash flow comes from the commodity price rather than from the
company. Three consequences follow for how the page is read:

- **Single-year multiples mislead.** P/E and P/FCF look cheapest at a price
  peak, when earnings are about to fall, and most expensive at a trough, when
  earnings are temporarily depressed or negative. Each ratio is compared with
  its own median across at least one full cycle, and never read from one year.
- **Reported earnings carry large non-cash items.** Foreign exchange on US
  dollar debt, mark-to-market on hedges, share-based compensation marked to the
  share price, remeasurement gains on acquisitions and recoverability charges
  inside depletion can each move net earnings by billions. The page notes these
  in the year they occur, and leans on free cash flow when they distort
  earnings.
- **Volume is the part management controls.** Production per share separates
  what the company achieved (more barrels for each share outstanding) from what
  the market gave it (a higher price for each barrel).

## What we measure

### 1. Valuation through the cycle

| Metric      | Calculation                                    | What to watch for                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| P/E ratio   | Year-end price ÷ diluted EPS                   | Compare it with its own median over a full cycle. A low P/E in a year of high prices is a warning rather than a bargain, because earnings are likely to revert. |
| P/FCF ratio | Year-end price ÷ free cash flow per share      | Less distorted by non-cash items than P/E. Read the forward year against the commodity prices the consensus assumes.                                            |
| Reverse DCF | The growth the price implies at a 10% discount | Ask whether the implied growth needs prices above today's strip or volumes beyond what the reserves can supply.                                                 |

### 2. Production and reserves per share

| Metric                   | Calculation                                                   | What to watch for                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Production per share     | Average daily production before royalties ÷ diluted shares    | Should rise steadily. Growth bought with newly issued shares, or volume added by acquisitions at high prices, shows up here as weaker than headline growth. |
| Reserve life index       | Proved (or proved plus probable) reserves ÷ annual production | Long-life assets such as oil sands mines need little reinvestment to hold production flat, which is what makes the free cash flow durable.                  |
| Capital spending vs. DDA | Capital expenditures ÷ depletion, depreciation & amortization | Spending persistently below depletion means the asset base is being harvested; well above it means growth or rising finding costs.                          |

### 3. Returns and margins

| Metric           | Calculation                                                                                     | What to watch for                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Return on equity | Net earnings ÷ year-end shareholders' equity                                                    | Average it across the cycle. A producer that earns its cost of equity only at peak prices is not compounding value.                                       |
| ROIC             | (Earnings before taxes + interest) × (1 − statutory tax rate) ÷ (equity + debt + leases − cash) | Shows returns on the whole capital base, so it is not flattered by leverage.                                                                              |
| FCF margin       | Free cash flow ÷ revenue                                                                        | Revenue is net of royalties. A margin that holds up in weak-price years points to a low cost structure and to assets that need little sustaining capital. |

### 4. Balance sheet and distributions

| Metric                  | Calculation                                         | What to watch for                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Debt to equity          | Long-term debt (including current portion) ÷ equity | Debt taken on for acquisitions at a price peak is the most common way producers destroy value. Watch how fast it falls afterwards.            |
| Dividends per share     | Dividends declared per share, split-adjusted        | A dividend raised through a price trough signals that it is set on the low-cost base rather than on the price. Special dividends are flagged. |
| Sustainable growth rate | ROE × (1 − dividends ÷ net earnings)                | Meaningful only in years with positive earnings, and only as a cycle average.                                                                 |

## What we do not do

- **No commodity price forecast.** The page shows what consensus and
  management assume, not a view on where oil or gas prices will go.
- **No net asset value model.** Reserve-based NAV depends on a long-run price
  deck and a discount rate that the page cannot verify, so reserves are used as
  a life index rather than as a valuation.
- **No quarterly tracking.** Figures are fiscal-year snapshots, and quarterly
  price noise is omitted deliberately.
