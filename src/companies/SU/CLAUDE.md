# SU — Suncor Energy Inc.

Analytical method: [energy](../../methods/energy.md)

The page renders the software template, so its section names follow that
template. The critical metrics replace P/FCF with price to free funds flow and
add EV / AFFO, as the energy method describes.

## Company-specific notes

- **Currency and listing.** Suncor reports in Canadian dollars under IFRS and
  files a 40-F with the SEC each year. Prices are TSX closes in Canadian
  dollars, so no currency conversion is needed. Benchmark oil prices are in US
  dollars.

- **Fiscal year.** Calendar year ending 31 December, so "FY25" covers January
  through December 2025.

- **Revenue line.** The page uses total revenues and other income, which is
  net of royalties. Before FY22 the income statement showed only operating
  revenues net of royalties; from FY22 it shows gross revenues less royalties.
  The total is the same concept in both. Gross revenues by segment and
  royalties are in the Revenue Streams section. FY17 figures are the restated
  comparatives from the FY18 statements, which moved CA$97M between gross
  revenues and costs.

- **Operating income.** IFRS statements have no operating income line. The
  page uses earnings before financing expenses and income taxes. Financing
  expenses include unrealized foreign exchange on US dollar debt, which made
  them a net gain in FY17 and a CA$2.14B expense in FY18.

- **Free funds flow.** Suncor's own measure is adjusted funds from operations
  (operating cash flow before changes in non-cash working capital) minus capital
  and exploration expenditures. It ignores the large working capital swings
  that follow oil prices, so the page uses it for P/FFF, FFF per share and the
  reverse DCF. The FY22 and FY23 figures follow Suncor in leaving out spending
  on assets held for sale; the Cash Flow section's free cash flow includes it.

- **Net debt.** The page counts lease liabilities as debt, because free funds
  flow is not reduced by lease payments. Suncor's own net debt, as restated in
  the FY25 MD&A, excludes leases (CA$6.34B at the end of FY25 against CA$10.85B
  here).

- **Impairments.** Fort Hills was impaired in FY19, FY20 and FY22, and White
  Rose and Terra Nova in FY20. They sit in depreciation, depletion,
  amortization and impairment, and depress EPS and ROE in those years.

- **Portfolio changes.** Suncor bought an additional 5% of Syncrude in FY18,
  sold the lubricants business and its Cedar Point wind interest in FY17, sold
  the U.K. offshore portfolio and bought TotalEnergies Canada (the remaining
  Fort Hills interest) in FY23, and agreed in October 2026 to sell Terra Nova,
  White Rose and West White Rose to Ithaca Energy, with closing expected in
  early 2027.

- **Refinery capacity.** Nameplate capacity rose from 466,000 to 511,000
  bbls/d on 1 January 2026, so utilization from 2026 is not comparable with
  earlier years and the FY26E guidance is left off that panel.

- **Forward estimates.** Suncor gives no EPS or free funds flow guidance, so
  FY26E EPS (CA$12.11, adjusted), revenue (CA$66.55B), net earnings
  (CA$14.42B) and free funds flow (CA$14.47B) are the consensus on
  stockanalysis.com (S&P Global, 22 October 2026). Production, refinery
  throughput, capital spending and cash operating costs are midpoints of the
  2026 corporate guidance of 11 December 2025, whose ranges were reaffirmed on
  4 August 2026. FY26E operating income is derived from consensus net earnings
  at the guided 24.5% Canadian tax rate plus first-half financing expenses
  doubled. Per-share estimates use the 1,184M diluted shares of the first half
  of 2026. The forward P/E, P/FFF and EV / AFFO recalculate when the user
  adjusts the price.

- **Oil price context.** WTI averaged US$64.75 in FY25 and US$82.55 in the first
  half of 2026, so the FY26E figures are far above FY25. The reverse DCF shows
  a second path that holds free funds flow at the FY25 level.
