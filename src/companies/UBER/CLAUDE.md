# UBER — Uber Technologies, Inc.

Analytical method: [software](../../methods/software.md)

## Company-specific notes

- **Segments.** Uber reports three segments: Mobility (ride-hailing, rentals,
  transit), Delivery (Uber Eats, grocery, convenience), and Freight (freight
  brokerage via Uber Freight and Transplace). The ATG and Other Technology
  Programs segment was divested to Aurora Innovation in early 2021; segment
  data in this page starts from FY20.

- **Fiscal year.** Uber uses a calendar fiscal year ending 31 December, so
  "FY25" covers January through December 2025. All figures in the data files
  use fiscal-year labels.

- **Revenue recognition.** Uber reports revenue net of payments to drivers
  and couriers (the company retains the platform fee, not the gross booking).
  The "take rate" — revenue as a percentage of gross bookings — varies by
  segment and geography. In 2022, a UK court ruling reclassified UK drivers
  as workers, which shifted some UK Mobility revenue from a net to a gross
  basis and inflated reported Mobility revenue that year.

- **SG&A mapping.** Uber's income statement separates operating expenses into
  Operations and Support, Sales and Marketing, and General and Administrative.
  These three lines are combined into the single SG&A field in the financials
  data to match the template's expense structure.

- **Non-operating volatility.** Uber holds equity stakes in Aurora, Grab,
  Didi, and Joby Aviation. Mark-to-market changes on these investments cause
  large swings in net income that do not reflect operating performance — most
  notably a $7.5 billion loss in FY22 and gains in FY21 and FY23–FY24. The
  FY24 and FY25 net income figures also include multi-billion-dollar benefits
  from releasing deferred tax asset valuation allowances.
