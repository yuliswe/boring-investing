# MSFT — Microsoft Corporation

## Analytical method

The analysis uses a **franchise-quality** approach rather than a discounted-cash-flow
model. The goal is to assess whether Microsoft's core businesses have durable
competitive advantages that sustain high returns on capital over long periods, not to
arrive at a precise intrinsic value.

### What we measure

1. **Revenue growth by segment.** Each of Microsoft's three reporting segments
   (Productivity & Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, More Personal Computing) is
   tracked separately so that a slowdown in one segment does not hide behind strength
   in another.

2. **Operating margin trend.** A widening margin on growing revenue signals pricing
   power and operating leverage — both hallmarks of a franchise. A margin that
   compresses while revenue grows points to commoditisation or investment-heavy
   expansion whose returns are unproven.

3. **Free cash flow conversion.** High free cash flow relative to net income confirms
   that reported earnings translate into cash the company can return or reinvest.
   Persistent gaps between the two warrant investigation (capitalised leases, large
   stock-based compensation, working-capital swings).

4. **Capital allocation.** Buybacks, dividends, and acquisition spending are noted
   qualitatively. The question is whether management reinvests at returns above the
   cost of capital or distributes excess cash, not whether one policy is inherently
   better than the other.

### What we do not do

- **No DCF or target price.** The page presents data and a qualitative thesis, not a
  valuation or a buy/sell recommendation.
- **No quarterly tracking.** Figures are fiscal-year snapshots. Quarterly noise is
  omitted deliberately.
- **No peer comparison.** Each company is assessed on its own merits. Cross-company
  comparison belongs to a screen, not a company page.
