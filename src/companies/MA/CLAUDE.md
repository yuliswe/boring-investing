# MA — Mastercard Incorporated

Analytical method: [software](../../methods/software.md)

## Company-specific notes

- **Business model.** Mastercard operates a two-sided payment network connecting
  cardholders and merchants through issuing and acquiring banks. Revenue comes
  from assessment fees (a percentage of gross dollar volume), transaction
  processing fees (per switch), cross-border volume fees, and value-added
  services. Mastercard does not issue cards, extend credit, or set interest
  rates, so it carries no consumer credit risk.

- **Segments.** Mastercard reports two revenue lines: Payment Network (domestic
  and cross-border assessment fees, transaction processing) and Value-Added
  Services and Solutions (cyber and intelligence, data and services, loyalty,
  processing, and real-time payments). VAS grew from roughly a third of revenue
  in FY21 to over 40% in FY25.

- **Fiscal year.** Calendar fiscal year ending 31 December, so "FY25" covers
  January through December 2025.

- **Stock split.** Mastercard completed a 10-for-1 stock split on 22 January 2014. All per-share figures and share counts in the data files are post-split.

- **FY17 TCJA charge.** GAAP net income and EPS for FY17 were depressed by an
  $873 million provisional charge for the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (transition tax
  on accumulated foreign earnings). This is a one-time non-cash charge that
  makes FY17 P/E appear elevated relative to underlying earnings power.

- **FY18 litigation provision.** FY18 operating expenses include a $1,128
  million provision for litigation related to the U.S. merchant class action
  settlement, compressing operating income and net income for that year. Without
  this provision, FY18 operating margin would have been approximately 56%
  instead of the reported 49%.

- **COVID-19 impact.** FY20 revenue fell 9% as cross-border travel and in-person
  spending declined sharply. The recovery in FY21 was strong, with revenue
  exceeding FY19 levels.
