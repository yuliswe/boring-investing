# BRK — Berkshire Hathaway Inc.

Analytical method: [software](../../methods/software.md)

## Company-specific notes

- **Business model.** Berkshire Hathaway is a diversified holding company that
  owns a portfolio of wholly owned operating businesses (insurance, railroad,
  energy, manufacturing, services, retailing) and a concentrated public equity
  portfolio. Revenue comes from insurance premiums, freight transportation,
  energy generation and distribution, manufacturing and retailing, and
  investment income. Capital allocation is centralised at the holding company
  level.

- **Share classes.** BRK-A (Class A) and BRK-B (Class B) shares are
  economically equivalent at a 1,500:1 ratio. All per-share figures on this
  page are stated in BRK-B terms. Total BRK-B equivalent diluted shares
  declined from approximately 2.47 billion in FY17 to 2.16 billion in FY25
  through share buybacks.

- **Revenue metric.** The page uses operating revenue (total revenues excluding
  investment and derivative contract gains/losses) as the top-line revenue
  figure. GAAP total revenue includes unrealized investment gains and losses,
  which have been required to flow through the income statement since FY18
  (FASB ASU 2016-01). Including them would make the revenue line as volatile
  as GAAP net income and unsuitable for trend analysis.

- **Operating earnings.** Berkshire reports "operating earnings" (after-tax
  earnings excluding investment/derivative gains/losses and impairment charges)
  as its primary performance metric. The `operatingIncome` field in the revenue
  data is populated with this after-tax figure, not a GAAP pre-tax operating
  income line. GAAP net income is shown separately and includes the volatile
  investment gains/losses.

- **Valuation metrics.** GAAP P/E is meaningless for Berkshire because GAAP
  earnings swing from negative to nearly $100B depending on equity market
  movements. The critical metrics use P/B ratio (the traditional Berkshire
  valuation anchor, with a historical buyback threshold near 1.2x book) and
  P/Operating Earnings (the best earnings-based valuation metric).

- **Fiscal year.** Calendar fiscal year ending 31 December, so "FY25" covers
  January through December 2025.

- **FY17 hurricane losses.** Insurance underwriting reported a $2.2B loss in
  FY17 from Hurricanes Harvey, Irma, and Maria, depressing operating earnings
  and inflating the P/Operating Earnings ratio for that year.

- **FY18 FASB ASU 2016-01.** Beginning in FY18, unrealized gains and losses on
  equity securities are included in GAAP net income rather than other
  comprehensive income. This is the single largest driver of GAAP earnings
  volatility: the equity portfolio (dominated by Apple) generated swings of +$62B (FY21) to -$54B (FY22) in after-tax investment gains/losses.

- **FY20 PCP impairment.** GAAP net income for FY20 includes a $10.9B
  after-tax goodwill impairment charge on Precision Castparts, acquired in
  2016, reflecting the impact of COVID-19 on the aerospace supply chain.

- **FY23 Pilot consolidation.** Berkshire completed its acquisition of the
  remaining 20% of Pilot Travel Centers in January 2023, consolidating Pilot's
  approximately $50B in annual revenue into Berkshire's sales and service
  revenues line. This accounts for the 20.6% jump in operating revenue from
  FY22 to FY23.

- **FY25 KHC and OXY impairments.** GAAP net income for FY25 includes $8.3B
  in after-tax impairment charges related to Kraft Heinz and Occidental
  Petroleum equity investments.

- **Cash flow structure.** Berkshire's cash flow statement differs from most
  companies because investing activities are dominated by purchases and sales
  of equity and fixed-income securities (not by capital expenditures). Capital
  expenditures ($12-21B per year) are a small fraction of operating cash flow
  ($31-49B), so free cash flow (operating minus capex) remains meaningful.

- **No dividends, no guidance.** Berkshire does not pay a dividend and does not
  provide earnings guidance. Analyst coverage is extremely thin (typically 1-2
  analysts). Forward estimates should be treated with caution.
