# NVDA — NVIDIA Corporation

Analytical method: [software](../../methods/software.md)

## Company-specific notes

- **Fiscal year.** NVIDIA uses a 52/53-week fiscal year ending on the last
  Sunday in January, so "FY26" covers approximately February 2025 through
  January 25, 2026. All figures in the data files use NVIDIA's fiscal-year
  labels.

- **Stock splits.** NVIDIA executed a 4-for-1 stock split in July 2021 and
  a 10-for-1 stock split in June 2024. All per-share figures are
  retroactively adjusted for both splits (i.e. divided by 40 relative to
  pre-split shares).

- **Segment structure.** NVIDIA reports revenue by five market platforms:
  Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, Automotive, and OEM &
  Other. The segment data begins at FY17. Data Center revenue has grown
  from roughly 12% of total revenue in FY17 to 90% in FY26, driven by
  AI accelerator demand.

- **Mellanox acquisition.** NVIDIA acquired Mellanox Technologies in
  April 2020 (FY21) for approximately $7 billion, adding high-speed
  networking products to the Data Center platform. This increased FY21
  revenue and operating expenses relative to FY20.

- **ARM deal termination.** In FY23, NVIDIA recorded a $1.35 billion
  acquisition termination cost related to the cancelled acquisition of
  Arm Holdings. This charge depressed GAAP operating income and net income
  in FY23.

- **FY23 downturn.** FY23 saw a significant drop in Gaming revenue (from
  $12.46B to $9.07B) due to post-COVID demand normalization and a crypto
  mining inventory glut, combined with the ARM termination charge, resulting
  in much lower operating income ($4.22B vs $10.04B in FY22).

- **AI inflection.** Beginning in FY24, Data Center revenue accelerated
  sharply as hyperscale cloud providers and enterprises adopted NVIDIA's
  H100 and subsequent GPU architectures for AI training and inference.
  Revenue more than doubled in each of FY24 and FY25.
