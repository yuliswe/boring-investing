# ENE — Enron Corp.

Analytical method: [software](../../methods/software.md)

## Company-specific notes

- **Defunct company.** Enron Corp. filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on 2
  December 2001 and was subsequently liquidated. The page covers the final
  ten fiscal years (FY1992 through FY2001) using figures as reported at the
  time of each filing. There is no forward estimate and no price adjuster,
  because the stock is worthless.

- **Revenue recognition.** Enron adopted mark-to-market accounting for its
  energy trading contracts beginning in the early 1990s, recording the full
  notional value of long-term contracts as revenue at inception. From FY1997
  onward, Enron reported gross trading volumes as revenue rather than the net
  margin, inflating reported revenue from single-digit billions to over $100B
  by FY2000. The revenue figures on this page are as filed, which means
  they are not comparable to how most companies report.

- **November 2001 restatement.** On 8 November 2001, Enron restated its
  financial statements for FY1997 through the first half of FY2001, reducing
  previously reported net income by approximately $600M across those years
  and increasing reported debt by $2.6B through the consolidation of
  off-balance-sheet entities (Chewco, JEDI, LJM). Where restated figures
  are available, this page uses them.

- **Off-balance-sheet entities.** Enron used special purpose entities (SPEs)
  to move debt off its balance sheet, hedge losses on merchant investments,
  and manufacture operating cash flow through "prepay" transactions. The
  most notorious vehicles were Chewco (managed by CFO Andrew Fastow's
  subordinate Michael Kopper), JEDI, LJM1, and LJM2 (managed directly by
  Fastow). These entities are the reason Enron's reported debt-to-equity
  ratio understated the company's true leverage.

- **Cash flow manipulation.** Enron structured commodity prepay transactions
  with banks (primarily JPMorgan Chase and Citigroup) as financing
  arrangements that appeared as operating cash flow on Enron's books. The
  bankruptcy examiner estimated that $5B or more in what should have been
  reported as financing flows was instead classified as operating cash flow,
  making Enron appear to generate far more cash from operations than it
  actually did.

- **Fiscal year.** Calendar fiscal year ending 31 December.

- **Stock price.** ENE traded on the NYSE. The stock peaked at approximately
  $90 in August 2000, closed FY2000 at approximately $83, and was trading
  below $1 by late November 2001. The hero section shows the year-end 2000
  price as the reference.

- **Auditor.** Arthur Andersen LLP audited all Enron 10-K filings shown on
  this page. Andersen was convicted of obstruction of justice in June 2002
  for shredding Enron audit documents and subsequently surrendered its CPA
  licenses, though the conviction was later overturned by the Supreme Court.

- **FY2001.** Enron never filed a 10-K for FY2001. Financial data for that
  year comes from the 10-Q filings for Q1-Q3 2001, the November 2001
  restatement disclosures, and the bankruptcy examiner's report.
