# KO — The Coca-Cola Company

Analytical method: [software](../../methods/software.md)

## Company-specific notes

- **Fiscal year.** Coca-Cola's fiscal year ends on 31 December, so "FY25"
  is calendar 2025. Its interim quarters end on Fridays (for example 3 July
  2026), but the annual figures are always calendar years.

- **Business model.** Coca-Cola mostly sells concentrate and syrups to
  independent bottlers, and it holds equity stakes in many of them. Equity
  income from those stakes ($2.0B in FY25) sits below operating income, so
  operating margin and ROIC understate the economics of the system, and the
  non-operating line in the cost analysis is negative (a net addition to
  earnings) in every year from FY19.

- **Refranchising.** Revenue falls whenever Coca-Cola sells a company-owned
  bottler to a partner, while margins rise. The North America refranchising
  ended in FY17–FY18; the Philippines, Bangladesh, and parts of India were
  refranchised in FY24; 40% of the India bottler was sold in July 2025; and
  the sale of the Africa bottler is pending (expected to close in the third
  or fourth quarter of 2026). Read revenue declines in Bottling Investments
  as structural rather than demand-driven.

- **Segment break.** Global Ventures (Costa, innocent, doğadan, and Monster
  fees) was a separate segment from FY19 until 1 January 2025. The FY25 10-K
  recasts FY23–FY24 into the geographic segments, but FY17–FY22 are only
  available as originally filed with Global Ventures separate. The segment
  chart therefore shows Global Ventures through FY22 and a step-up in EMEA in
  FY23 that is a reclassification. Segment figures are third-party revenue,
  which sums to consolidated revenue without eliminations.

- **FY17 tax charge.** The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act charge put the FY17
  effective tax rate at 81.4% and EPS at $0.29, so FY17 P/E, sustainable
  growth, and cash earnings quality are shown as `null`.

- **FY24–FY25 cash flow.** Operating cash flow absorbed the $6.0B deposit
  paid to the IRS in FY24 (the 2007–2009 transfer-pricing case, where the Tax
  Court entered a decision of $2.7B of additional federal tax plus interest)
  and $6.1B of the $6.2B final fairlife milestone payment in FY25. Free cash
  flow per share, P/FCF, FCF margin, and cash earnings quality are distorted
  in both years. The fairlife contingent consideration was remeasured through
  other operating charges each year, including $3.1B in FY24.

- **Debt definition.** Total debt is loans and notes payable plus current
  maturities of long-term debt plus long-term debt. FY18 uses the FY19 10-K
  balance sheet, which reclassified some current maturities.

- **FY26E basis.** Revenue is the stockanalysis.com consensus ($49.72B,
  30 September 2026), because Coca-Cola guides only growth rates for revenue.
  EPS ($3.29) applies the midpoint of comparable (non-GAAP) EPS growth
  guidance of 9% to 10% (28 July 2026) to FY25 comparable EPS of $3.00, so
  FY26E P/E is not comparable with the GAAP history. Free cash flow ($12.4B),
  operating cash flow ($14.6B), and capital expenditures ($2.2B) are company
  guidance. Replace FY26E with the FY26 10-K figures once filed (expected
  February 2027).

- **Open items.** The IRS case was on appeal at the Eleventh Circuit as of the
  FY25 10-K, the fairlife plants in
  the United States were shut by a ransomware attack in July 2026, and the
  Africa bottler sale will shrink Bottling Investments again when it closes.
